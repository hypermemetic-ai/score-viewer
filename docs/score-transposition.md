# Score transposition

Open a score, then open **Options** (O by default). **Score** is the first section, or follows **Practice** when practising. Transpose is measured in **steps**: positive raises every pitch, negative lowers it, and 0 restores the retained original. One step is the next adjacent chromatic pitch. The legal range depends on the score's highest and lowest sounding pitches; pitches never wrap or fold into another octave.

The numeric field uses the same controls as practice speed. Arrows browse Options. Enter edits the value; type a whole number or use Up/Down, then Enter commits or Escape cancels. The stacked mouse chevrons are outside keyboard navigation. An invalid draft leaves the score unchanged. During preparation, Cancel keeps the current edition and value. The new value becomes selected only after complete admission and additive durable installation.

Reading, practice and performance assessment use the same selected pitches. The physical piano still sounds the incoming MIDI pitches: this setting changes the score, rather than the piano's global input. Original reference MIDI remains an original-source attachment; the viewer has no score-MIDI playback feature.

The viewer does not calculate fingers or hands. A new pitch edition is engraved from the transposed source with the shared production rules and keeps the score's own hands on both keyboards. It carries no edition finger layer: the fingerings printed with an edition belong to its pitches, so they stay with the score at its original pitch. The player's own hand and finger choices move with their notes when a note keeps its identity and timing and its pitch moved by the same steps; older finger corrections move as finger choices. The notice after transposing says how many notes' choices came along and how many could not move and stay with the earlier edition. A score with notes that have no hand in the source cannot be transposed yet; no hand is supplied for them.

Each pitch edition has its own practice and performance evidence. Achieving the original pitch does not count as achieving the shifted exercise. Original archives, choices and journals remain retained. Selecting a retained pitch edition, including one the former fingering calculator made, recovers its own saved choices and progress without recalculating it. A retained pitch edition whose pages no longer reproduce is engraved again at its pitch from its own hands and fingers; see [saved-edition-reengraving.md](saved-edition-reengraving.md). Selecting 0 restores the exact retained untransposed archive for the selected keyboard.

Prepared journal keys include the exact source collection, keyboard, profile, hand map and edition. Their physical filenames use a SHA-256 alias of that complete key, within the unchanged native managed-file limit. Logical library pointers and journal content bindings remain unchanged. Existing original filenames and versioned personal pointers remain exact; older browser records with long raw keys are still readable and retained. New writes use the bounded alias. This closes the native missing-journal lookup failure found after the first transposed keyboard preparation; source validation of this storage boundary does not establish Windows activation or physical playing acceptance.

When changing pitch from practice, the old session is paused and its save queue is settled before installation. The new session keeps the passage range, grouping structure, legacy partition, browsing choice and applicable Free view window. A fresh edition inherits practice preferences, including starting speed, increase, target and speed lock, but begins at starting speed without earned progress. A retained edition keeps its own saved preferences and progress. It starts paused with new pitch obligations and without inherited completion eligibility. Old queued MIDI events cannot advance its assessment. Opening Options and cancelling an edit retains the existing session.

The selected offset belongs to the original score collection and survives reopening and keyboard changes. Archives for each offset are retained independently. A new pitch edition serves both keyboards from one archive, so changing keyboard while transposed opens the same edition. Retained pitch editions made by the former calculator keep their own per-keyboard archives. Returning to 0 preserves the user's keyboard preference.

The target practice journal and grouping are validated before changing the selected archive. Retained grouping has priority when old navigation joins cross a differently joined target passage. Compatible joins are kept; if the exact previous range is unavailable, practice asks the player to choose a passage. This preserves the retained journal rather than inventing overlapping practice evidence.

A shared transposed archive contains its exact untransposed baseline once. Import stages that baseline additively so 0 also works offline on the receiving computer. Earlier editions retain their actual admitted offset; importing an original update while a nonzero offset is selected asks the player to restore 0 first instead of silently resetting the selection.

The archive authority and admission rules are documented in [score-transposition-authority.md](score-transposition-authority.md). Source tests and browser inspection establish source behavior; native recipient inspection and physical playing remain separate acceptance steps.
