# Score transposition authority

Viewer transposition is an additive derivation, not a mutation of the imported
score. `viewer-score-transposition-v1` retains one exact, independently admitted
untransposed archive at `transposition/original.isoscore`. A retained baseline
with a transposition envelope is refused before nested admission. The new
edition is engraved afresh with the current engine; it adopts the baseline's
page and spacing settings when it has a rendering recipe, and the baseline's
old pages need not reproduce first. This member
is the portable exact 0 restoration archive; the collection installer preserves
its original sidecar and achievement journals separately.

The ordinary source-score member is the deterministic effective source. Its
source SHA, layer source identity and rendering recipe bind the effective
pitches through existing strict checks. The envelope separately binds the root
source SHA, baseline archive SHA/layout, integer offset, effective source SHA
and the SHA of the edition it was made from. The transposition runs in the
reader; no worker result is trusted. No field is added to ISO's strictly
tempo-only calculation context.

The complete sorted owner hand map is guarded by original owner ID, pitch,
onset and duration. A new pitch edition's map is the original score's own
hands, the same for both keyboards. Cold import admits the untransposed
baseline and all original owner guards, reconstructs the effective source, and
checks that the edition's hands equal the map. Its pages are engraved with the
shared production rules when the edition is made and are then read as saved,
with seats checked against them. It does not
establish historical user intent, anatomical suitability or continuous movement.

The viewer no longer calculates fingers or hands, so no new edition carries a
finger layer, a preparation identity or a calculated-hand receipt. Retained
transposed editions the former calculator made keep preparation V4 and
calculated-hand receipt V3, bound to the complete transposition envelope and
the pinned annotation-ink policy, and are still replayed on admission.
Historical preparation V1/V2/V3 and receipt V1/V2 retain their literal meaning
and never acquire a transposition exemption.

Absolute note, repeat-tie, pitched grace, supported occurrence-override and
source-hand-guard pitches shift together, without clipping or octave folding.
Relative/reading source projections and unfamiliar pitch-bearing fields refuse.
Timing, owner IDs, source relation identities and non-pitch provenance remain
unchanged. Physical pitches are ISO coordinates 9–96, corresponding to A0–C8.

The selected tempo context is retained and rebound to the effective
source hash; ISO's source-reference conflict rules remain in force. Reference
MIDI is an exact original-source attachment, independently compared against the
retained baseline. It is not transposed playback: the current viewer only admits
that attachment. Live piano MIDI passthrough still sounds incoming physical keys.

A new pitch edition starts its own journal. The player's hand and finger
choices whose notes keep their identity, timing and moved pitch are carried into
it as choices with the moved pitch guard; the edition's printed fingerings and
achievements are not. The earlier edition's journal is unchanged. Retained-offset
reuse requires the exact root baseline, keyboard and reference context; a
retained calculated edition must also match the hands now shown, rather than
trusting an offset or an internally consistent archive alone.
